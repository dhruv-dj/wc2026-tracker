🎲 How the odds work

📊

Step 1 — Team strength

Every team starts with a base strength rating derived from FIFA world rankings and historical World Cup performance. Argentina (defending champions) sit at the top, followed by France, Spain, and Portugal.

📈

Step 2 — Live adjustment

As matches are played, ratings shift based on actual results. A team earning more points per game than expected gains rating; one that underperforms loses it. Goal difference adds a further nudge — a 4-0 win moves the needle more than a 1-0 squeaker.

🎯

Step 3 — Match simulation

Head-to-head win probability uses a logistic model: a 10-point rating gap translates to roughly a 60/40 advantage. Draw probability shrinks as the skill gap widens. In knockouts there are no draws — penalty-shootout randomness is baked in by compressing odds slightly toward 50/50.

🔁

Step 4 — Monte Carlo (8 000 runs)

The entire remaining tournament — every unplayed group game, all 32 knockout matches — is simulated 8 000 times from the current state. The percentage shown is simply how often each team lifted the trophy across those runs. More simulations = smoother numbers; 8 000 keeps the page snappy while staying accurate to within ~1%.

⏱️

Updates

GitHub Actions re-runs the full simulation every 3 hours, pulling fresh scores from football-data.org. The page auto-refreshes every 10 minutes. Odds after a major upset can shift dramatically — check back after big games.

⚠️

Caveats

This is a fun model, not a betting system. It doesn't account for injuries, suspensions, or tactical matchups. The WC 2026 format (48 teams, 12 groups, 8 best third-place teams advancing) makes early-stage odds especially noisy — they stabilise once knockout brackets are set.
